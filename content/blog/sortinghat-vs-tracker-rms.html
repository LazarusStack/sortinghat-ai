The three questions that actually decide this

Comparison tables inform the decision. They rarely make it. Three questions do, and all three are about your firm rather than either platform.

What is capping your revenue?

Not what is annoying. What is capping revenue. If your recruiters cannot carry more roles, the answer is throughput, and throughput comes from removing manual sourcing and screening rather than from better record keeping. Firms routinely buy for the annoyance and change nothing.

What happens to the team in week one?

The most common failure in this category is not a bad platform. It is a good platform nobody switched to. Ask both vendors what happens in week one, week four and week twelve, who does the migration, and what it costs. A vague answer is the answer.

What does the all-in number look like at year three?

Licence plus implementation plus migration plus every module you have to add to reach a working setup plus renewal escalation. Then subtract what the platform removes from recruiter hours and job portal spend. Vendors who will only discuss the per-seat headline are telling you something about the other five lines.

What to test before you decide

Five things that separate a real evaluation from a feature tour. Run them on both platforms with your own data, not the vendor's demo database.

  1. Search your own database in plain English. Describe someone you actually placed and see whether they appear in the top twenty.
  2. Open a candidate score and ask why. If the answer is a number with no reasoning behind it, your recruiters will ignore the ranking.
  3. Look at one candidate contacted on three channels. One timeline, or three separate logs?
  4. Ask what the all-in price is, including every module you would actually license. Then ask what it renews at.
  5. Ask for the migration plan in writing, with anything that will not carry across named explicitly.

What a migration should actually include

Migration is the reason most firms stay on a platform they outgrew, so it is worth knowing what good looks like before you ask any vendor about it.

Notes, calls, attachments and submission history. These are the records most migrations leave behind and the ones that make a database worth keeping. Ask explicitly rather than assuming, because "we migrate your data" and "we migrate your history" are different promises.

Field mapping done by the vendor. An export file and a spreadsheet is not a migration. Somebody has to decide what maps where, and it should not be your operations lead in the evenings.

A validation environment before go-live. Your team should review the migrated data and sign it off, rather than discovering gaps in week three when a client asks about a candidate.
