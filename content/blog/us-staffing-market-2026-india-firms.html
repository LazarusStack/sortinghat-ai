The US staffing market was worth roughly $189 billion in 2024 and unemployment has hovered near 4.3 percent through 2026. Those two numbers are frequently used to argue opposite cases: that the market is enormous, and that hiring has stalled. Both are true and neither tells an Indian firm what to do, because what matters is which segments are moving and which buyer is doing the moving.

Last reviewed July 2026. US market figures from Staffing Industry Analysts and US Bureau of Labor Statistics reporting.

Key takeaways

  • The headline market size is not the addressable one. Roughly $189 billion covers everything from light industrial to executive search, most of which an offshore firm cannot serve.
  • Low unemployment does not mean easy hiring. It means the people you want are employed, which makes outbound and database depth the whole game.
  • Contingent demand behaves differently from permanent. Client caution on headcount pushes work towards contract, which is where offshore delivery competes best.
  • Your competition is not other Indian firms. It is the client's internal talent team and a US agency with a local relationship.

What the market size actually includes

The $189 billion figure covers the entire US staffing industry: light industrial, healthcare, clerical, IT, professional and executive search. Most of it involves placing people who must be physically present, which an offshore delivery team cannot serve.

The addressable portion for an India-based firm is narrower and worth naming honestly. IT and engineering contract staffing, sourcing and screening delivered as a service to US agencies, and RPO or embedded delivery where the recruiter works US hours against a US client's process.

That is still a very large market. It is not $189 billion, and building a plan against the headline is how firms end up with a target nobody can explain.

Why low unemployment makes outbound the whole game

At around 4.3 percent unemployment, the people worth hiring already have jobs. That has three consequences for delivery.

Inbound quality collapses. The applicants who are actively looking are disproportionately the ones nobody else wanted. Job posting produces volume without producing candidates.

Response rates decide throughput. Reaching employed people is a channel problem before it is a persuasion problem, and the difference between a 40 percent and a 12 percent response is the difference between a viable desk and an unviable one.

Database depth beats reach. Someone who spoke to your firm eighteen months ago and did not move is a warmer contact than a stranger from a purchased list, and costs nothing to reach.

Where contract demand is going

Client caution on permanent headcount consistently pushes demand towards contingent labour, and 2026 has followed that pattern.

For an Indian firm this is the favourable half of the cycle. Contract roles are more specification-driven, less dependent on cultural fit assessment, and more tolerant of an offshore sourcing and screening layer. They also renew, which turns a placement into an annuity rather than an event.

The catch is that contract work in the US frequently runs through a vendor management system, and the margin on VMS work is structurally lower. A firm entering US contract staffing through a VMS panel is entering the most competitive and lowest-margin part of the market first.

Who you are actually competing against

Not other Indian suppliers, which is the assumption that produces price-led pitches.

The client's internal talent team. Increasingly capable, increasingly well-tooled, and the default option. Your submission timeline is compared against theirs.

A US agency with a local relationship. They have coffee with the hiring manager and you have a call at 9pm. That gap closes with responsiveness and specificity, not with rate.

The status quo. A large share of roles are not filled by anyone because the requirement got deprioritised. Speed is what converts those.

What travels well offshore and what does not

Travels well: sourcing, database work, initial screening against defined criteria, scheduling, submission preparation and reporting. All of it is process work with a clear standard.

Travels badly: selling a role to a reluctant senior candidate, reading a hiring manager's unstated concern, and anything requiring local market credibility.

The firms that do well split on that line deliberately rather than trying to prove they can do everything from Bengaluru. The ones that struggle are usually competing on rate for work that needed presence.

What this changes on a staffing desk

Market analysis is only useful if it changes something on Monday. Four practical consequences that follow from everything above.

Your prospect list is probably built from history

Most staffing firms sell to the accounts they already know, which means the client list reflects where demand was five years ago rather than where it is now. Rebuilding a prospect list against current hiring activity rather than past relationships is unglamorous and it is usually the highest-return week a founder can spend.

Speed matters more than it used to

In contingency and RPO, the firm that submits first usually gets paid. A recruiter working manually manages around 40 calls a day and finds three or four people worth submitting. Filtering before dialling changes that arithmetic: a pool search on a volume role surfaces around 1,200 relevant profiles, narrows to a ranked 300 contacted across call, WhatsApp and email in parallel, and produces roughly 10 submission-ready profiles in hours.

Your own database is the cheapest source you have

An audit of one firm's 3 million records found roughly 10 percent duplicates, 10 percent unusable and 80 percent real people whose records were simply out of date. Making that 80 percent findable took job portal spend from about $100 a role to $60. Most firms are paying to source strangers while sitting on people who already know them.

Definition beats effort

Most wasted submissions are a briefing failure that surfaced three weeks later, not a sourcing failure. Generating weighted evaluation criteria from the job description, and agreeing them with the client before the search starts, removes more waste than any increase in activity.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the US staffing market?

Roughly $189 billion in 2024 according to Staffing Industry Analysts, covering light industrial, healthcare, clerical, IT, professional and executive search. The portion addressable from an offshore delivery base is considerably narrower.

Is now a good time to sell staffing services into the US?

Client caution on permanent headcount pushes demand towards contingent labour, which is the segment offshore delivery serves best. The constraint is that contract work frequently runs through vendor management systems where margins are lower.

What does low US unemployment mean for recruiters?

That the people worth hiring already have jobs. Inbound applicant quality falls, outbound response rates become the constraint on throughput, and an existing database of people who already know you becomes more valuable than reach into strangers.

Who do Indian staffing firms compete with in the US?

Primarily the client's internal talent team and US agencies with local relationships, rather than other offshore suppliers. Competing on rate against those two misreads what they are actually being chosen for.

What recruitment work can be delivered from India?

Sourcing, database work, screening against defined criteria, scheduling, submission preparation and reporting. Selling a role to a reluctant senior candidate and reading a hiring manager's unstated concerns travel considerably less well.

Is US contract staffing worth entering through a VMS?

It is the most accessible route and also the lowest-margin part of the market. Entering there first means competing on rate before you have built any relationship that would let you compete on anything else.

The number to check before you plan

Take your last twenty US submissions and work out how many came from your own database versus a fresh source. If the answer is mostly fresh, you are buying candidates in the most expensive market in the world without an asset to show for it afterwards.

Then check your response rate by channel on US roles specifically. It will be lower than your India numbers, and knowing by how much is what tells you whether the desk is viable at your current fee.
