Loxo's pitch is a large external profile database built into the ATS, which helps on the narrow set of roles where the person you need has never heard of your firm. Two things push firms to look elsewhere: the pool is rented rather than owned, so nothing you surface through it transfers when you leave, and it does not screen anybody it finds. This page compares seven alternatives.

Last reviewed June 2026. Platform information taken from published pricing pages and product documentation.

Key takeaways

  • The database does not travel. Profiles surfaced through Loxo belong to Loxo. What transfers is only the records you created and worked.
  • Sourcing is half the job. Finding a candidate still leaves a recruiter making every qualifying call, at around 40 a day.
  • Your own database is usually the cheaper answer. Our audit of one firm's 3 million records found 80% were real, current people behind stale records.
  • Compare the tier you would actually buy. Entry pricing rarely includes the AI, automation or analytics most firms are evaluating on, and that is where quotes diverge.

Why firms look past Loxo

Rented reach resets. Everything surfaced through an external database stays with the vendor. A pool you build compounds with every search; a subscription resets the day you stop paying.

It does not screen. Sourcing surfaces the candidate. A recruiter then calls, verifies and qualifies every one, which is the actual ceiling on the desk.

Your own database goes unused. Attention follows the subscription, so the records your firm spent years building sit behind filter and keyword search while you pay to reach strangers.

No back office. Contract desks need pay-and-bill somewhere, which means a second system.

Every option, in detail

1. Sortinghat

Best for: firms whose constraint is recruiter capacity rather than record keeping.

An AI-native ATS and CRM with full front and back office. Plain-language search across your own database, AI voice and video screening against criteria generated from the job description, an AI notetaker on client and candidate calls, multichannel outbound campaigns, pay-and-bill, timesheets and invoicing, and 400+ pre-built integrations with an open read-write API.

A pool search on a volume role surfaces around 1,200 relevant profiles, narrows to a ranked 300 contacted in parallel across call, WhatsApp and email, and produces roughly 10 submission-ready profiles in hours. On a high-volume QA requirement we submitted 120 profiles in a single day. Customers include firms of 400+ employees.

Migration takes two weeks with notes, calls, attachments and submission history carried across, and implementation, migration, training and any gap-filling customisation are included in the licence rather than quoted separately.

Where it is not the answer: if you renewed a multi-year agreement last quarter, or your team went live on something else in the last twelve months, the switching economics work better at your next renewal.

2. Crelate

Best for: recruiters who can already write a Boolean string

Boolean and proximity search with analytics, aimed at high-touch placement.

Watch for: The search rewards an expertise that is getting harder to hire for, which makes the desk dependent on one person's query-writing ability.

3. Recruit CRM

Best for: small teams who want a tidy system of record

A simple ATS and CRM that a small team adopts quickly. Candidates and clients in one place.

Watch for: It records what your recruiters did rather than doing any of it. Easy to adopt because there is not much to adopt.

4. Bullhorn

Best for: enterprise firms already committed to its ecosystem

The category incumbent since 1999, running more than 10,000 agencies. Assembled through a dozen acquisitions: Herefish became Bullhorn Automation, cube19 became Bullhorn Analytics, Textkernel and SourceBreaker became Amplify Search and Match, TargetRecruit arrived in 2025.

Watch for: Starter is $99 per user per month and Core is $165. The sales CRM sits on the quote-only Pro tier, and AI search, automation, analytics, SMS and onboarding are separately quoted add-ons. $165 is where it starts, not where a working setup lands.

5. Recruiterflow

Best for: agencies whose bottleneck is contacting people, not qualifying them

Multichannel outbound sequencing and workflow automation, with AI agents on its AIRA plan. It is built to get messages out at volume.

Watch for: Full AI functionality sits on the AIRA plan, so the capability most firms are comparing on depends on the tier you buy. It does not qualify anybody who replies.

6. Vincere

Best for: contract desks wanting front and back office in one system

Unified front and back office covering the contract lifecycle, strongest in APAC and the UK. Acquired by the Access Group.

Watch for: Pricing is not published. The integration solves reconciliation and does not raise recruiter capacity, and the roadmap now sits with an acquirer.

7. Manatal

Best for: teams whose only problem is ranking inbound CVs

An inexpensive ATS with AI candidate scoring on applications, reported at around $15 per user per month at entry.

Watch for: It ranks the applications you already received. If not enough qualified people are entering the funnel, scoring a thin pipeline changes nothing.

How to choose between them

If your desk is genuinely cold-market executive search, rented reach has a case and Loxo is a reasonable version of it.

If you want search precision and analytics, Crelate, provided your sourcers can write Boolean.

If you need back office, Vincere, Bullhorn or Sortinghat, which carries full pay-and-bill, timesheets and invoicing.

If most of the people you place are already somewhere in your database, which is the common case, the answer is a platform that makes your own records searchable and then screens them.

What to test before you shortlist anything

Five checks that separate a real evaluation from a feature tour. Run them on every platform with your own data rather than the vendor's demo database.

  1. Search your own database in plain English. Describe someone you actually placed and see whether they appear in the top twenty.
  2. Open a candidate score and ask why. A number with no reasoning behind it is a number your recruiters will ignore.
  3. Look at one candidate contacted on three channels. One timeline, or three separate logs?
  4. Ask for the all-in price, including every module you would actually license, then ask what it renews at.
  5. Ask for the migration plan in writing, with anything that will not carry across named explicitly.

Three questions that decide it faster than any comparison table

Shortlists inform the decision. These three make it, and all of them are about your firm rather than any platform.

What is capping your revenue?

Not what is annoying, what is capping revenue. If your recruiters cannot carry more roles, the answer is throughput, and throughput comes from removing manual sourcing and screening rather than from better record keeping. Firms routinely buy for the annoyance and change nothing.

What happens to the team in week one?

The most common failure in this category is not a bad platform. It is a good platform nobody switched to. Ask every vendor what happens in week one, week four and week twelve, who runs the migration, and what it costs. A vague answer is the answer.

What is the number at year three?

Licence plus implementation plus migration plus every module needed to reach a working setup plus renewal escalation, minus what the platform removes from recruiter hours and job portal spend. Vendors who will only discuss the per-seat headline are telling you something about the other five lines.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Loxo alternative?

For firms whose own database already holds most of the people they place, Sortinghat makes those records searchable and adds AI voice and video screening, generated evaluation criteria and full back office.

Does the Loxo database transfer if you leave?

No. Profiles surfaced through Loxo's own database belong to Loxo. Records you created and worked transfer, but the underlying pool does not.

How much does Loxo cost?

Loxo offers a free tier, with Basic reported around $169 per user per month and Professional quoted custom. Published figures vary across sources, so get a current written quote at your seat count.

Is a rented candidate database worth it?

On cold-market executive search, often yes. On roles where your firm has existing history, you are paying to reach strangers while your own records go unsearched.

Which alternatives include AI screening?

Very few natively. Recruiterflow offers AI agents on its AIRA plan for sequencing. Sortinghat includes AI voice and video screening in the platform.

Can you migrate from Loxo?

Yes, and the ATS side is straightforward. Notes, calls, attachments and submission history carry across in two weeks. The pool surfaced through Loxo's database stays with Loxo.

Bottom line

Loxo rents you access to strangers, and on a narrow set of roles that is worth paying for. On the roles most firms actually run, it is an expensive answer to a problem they do not have, and it stops at sourcing. Sortinghat makes the database you already own searchable in plain language, screens the people it finds with AI voice and video calls, evaluates them against criteria generated from the job description, and runs full front and back office, with 400+ pre-built integrations.
